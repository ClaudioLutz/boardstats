## Terms and slang
- Gold standard - a monetary system in which a currency's value is directly linked to a fixed quantity of gold.
- Fiat currency - money that has value by government decree rather than being backed by a physical commodity.
- Quantitative easing (QE) - a central bank policy of creating money to buy assets and inject liquidity into the economy.
- Bretton Woods / gold convertibility - the post-WWII arrangement (ended by Nixon in 1971) under which the US dollar was exchangeable for gold at a fixed rate and other currencies were pegged to the dollar.
- Petrodollar system - the practice of pricing/trading oil in US dollars, which increased global demand for dollars.
- Quantity theory of money - the theory that the general price level rises in proportion to the money supply.
- Quality theory of money - a competing theory holding that the consistency/purity ("quality") of money, not just its quantity, determines whether prices inflate.
- Troy ounce - a unit of mass (~31.1 grams) used for precious metals, distinct from the standard avoirdupois ounce.
- Goldbug / goldtard - slang, often derisive, for someone who strongly advocates returning to a gold-backed monetary system.
- Bagholder / baggie - slang for an investor left holding a losing or illiquid asset.
- Stacking / "stackoors" - slang for accumulating physical precious metals (e.g., gold/silver) as an investment.
- Nepo baby - slang for someone perceived as benefiting from family wealth/connections rather than merit.
- Peter Schiff - a financial commentator known for advocating gold and predicting fiat currency collapse, referenced here mockingly as shorthand for unsophisticated gold-standard arguments.
